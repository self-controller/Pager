# Small motions separate great iPhone reading apps

The best iPhone readers feel pleasant because they follow a short list of disciplined rules, not because of showy animation. They mostly converge on the same rules. The chrome leaves while you read and comes back only on a deliberate signal. One tap does exactly one thing. Every jump leaves a way back. Progress reads as "pages or minutes left in this chapter," and you can tap it to change the format. One tap highlights in the color you used last. Settings reflow the page live behind a half-height panel without losing your place. Motion is short and ease-out, roughly 80–500 ms, and haptics are used sparingly. The native apps publish almost no numbers. Apple Books, Kindle, YouVersion, Kobo and Libby document behavior, never durations. The exact values in this report therefore come from other sources: the companies' mobile web readers (bible.com, Substack, Medium), open-source readers (Omnivore, Readest, foliate-js), Libby's public web bundle, and Apple's own spring and haptics documentation. Every number is tagged with where it came from. The public record of reversals is the best evidence of what users care about. Apple restored the page curl, the brightness control and margins after iOS 16. Kindle's 2024 progress indicator was attacked for being tiny and laggy. Libby's tap-the-top-to-bookmark causes accidental bookmarks. Readwise Reader's changelog through October 2026 reads as a long fight against stray taps in a webview. Pager is built like Libby, Omnivore and Readest: a native shell around a web reader in WKWebView. So nearly every pattern here can be built in Pager's React layer. Native help is needed only for haptics, the edit menu, status-bar hiding, screen brightness, root bounce and back-swipe control. The highest-return work is not animation. It is five pieces: touch arbitration, a back-to-location pill, a tap-to-cycle footer, a haptics bridge, and a WKWebView subclass that adds Pager's items to the native edit menu.

## Most of the numbers come from web siblings and open-source readers

The native iOS apps publish behavior, never timing. Apple's Books user guide, YouVersion's help center, Kobo's and Libby's help pages, and press coverage of Kindle all describe what a tap or swipe does. None of the sources found in this research gives a duration, spring constant, tap-zone width or haptic event for Apple Books, Kindle, YouVersion, Kobo, Matter or Instapaper. The exact values come from other places, and the difference matters when Pager copies them. Every claim below carries one of these provenance tags:

| Tag | Meaning | Apps covered | How far to trust it |
|---|---|---|---|
| *[native]* | Behavior of the shipping iOS app, from help pages, release notes, press or user reports | Apple Books, Kindle, YouVersion, Kobo, Libby, Readwise Reader, Instapaper, Matter, Yomu, BookFusion | Says what happens; almost never says how fast |
| *[web]* | Measured in the company's own mobile web reader (CSS, minified JS, computed styles at 390 px) | YouVersion (bible.com), Substack, Medium | Exact values, but **the iOS app may differ** |
| *[code]* | Read in source code that ships in a real iOS app | Omnivore (SwiftUI + WKWebView, discontinued), Readest (Tauri, WKWebView), foliate-js, Libby's library web bundle | Exact values for that app; one team's choice, not a standard |
| *[Apple]* | Apple API documentation and WWDC sessions | SwiftUI and UIKit springs, bar behaviors, haptics guidelines | Authoritative for the APIs; Apple never publishes values for its own apps |
| *[RE]* | Community reverse engineering of UIKit physics | Scroll deceleration and rubber band | Several sources agree, but Apple does not document them |
| *[inference]* | My own reasoning or arithmetic | — | Needs a test on a device |

The *[code]* sources are closer to native truth than the *[web]* ones. Omnivore's and Readest's iOS apps run exactly the code that was read. Libby's library UI is the same "Dewey" web codebase that its native shells load ([Libby main.js](https://libbyapp.com/dewey-22.2.0/src/main.js)). By contrast, bible.com, Substack and Medium are web siblings of separate native apps. Some sources have limits worth knowing. The YouVersion screenshots were rendered locally from the downloaded HTML and CSS without JavaScript, because bible.com served a CAPTCHA. Their layout, color and spacing are real, but the typeface is a Linux fallback serif. The Substack and Medium screenshots were taken logged out, so they carry sign-up modals. Pocket shut down on 8 July 2025 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/05/22/mozilla-announces-shutdown-of-pocket/)), and this research found no inspectable record of its reader UI. Omnivore's cloud service ended in November 2024, but its iOS source is still public ([Omnivore README](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore)). That makes it the closest precedent to Pager: a SwiftUI shell, a bundled React reader and a `window.webkit.messageHandlers` bridge ([WebReader.swift](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/mobile/apple/OmnivoreKit/Sources/App/Views/WebReader/WebReader.swift)).

## Chrome leaves on a 10-point scroll and returns only on a deliberate pull

Every app studied hides its reading chrome. They differ in what brings it back, and that difference is where the feel comes from. In scroll views the dominant model is direction-based. **YouVersion's native iOS reader hides its top controls as you scroll into a chapter.** Its help tells users that if the ⋯ button is hidden, they should "scroll to the top of the chapter first" *[native]* ([YouVersion Help: iOS settings](https://help.youversion.com/l/en/article/j2nmx67p54-settings)). Substack's mobile web reader shows the mechanism in detail. The 57 px top bar reached `translateY(-57px)` after 400 px of downward scroll and came back on scroll-up. It has no CSS transition duration, so script drives it. The 57 px bottom like/comment/restack/share bar toggles a `hidden` class with **`transform 0.25s cubic-bezier(0.19, 1, 0.22, 1)`**, an ease-out-expo curve *[web]* ([Substack web reader](https://on.substack.com/p/the-tribe-has-spoken-how-drop-your)). Two smaller details make it feel considered. First, the bottom bar stays hidden at the very top of a post, because the same action row is already shown inline under the byline. Second, the toast viewport moves up 64 px while the bar is visible, so notifications never land under it *[web]*. Compare [substack_web_scrolldown_bars_hidden.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/substack_web_scrolldown_bars_hidden.png>) with [substack_web_scrollup_bars_visible.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/substack_web_scrollup_bars_visible.png>); both also show a logged-out subscribe modal. Medium's mobile web, by contrast, keeps a sticky 98 px header (logged out) and a 48 px sticky bottom bar that switches class on scroll-down. Its hide state could not be captured *[web]* ([Medium web reader](https://medium.com/blog/dark-mode-for-medium-is-finally-here-002977326982)).

Omnivore's iOS source gives a complete, shippable algorithm *[code]*. The reader records the scroll offset when a drag begins. At the top, or during overscroll, it shows the bar. It hides the bar once the offset passes the start-of-drag offset, and shows it again when a drag ends in an upward fling or the user taps the status bar. It moves `contentInset.top` together with the bar, so the first line never jumps under it. The bottom toolbar follows the nav bar, and a tap toggles both inside **`withAnimation(.easeIn(duration: 0.08))`**, an 80 ms change ([WebReaderCoordinator.swift](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/mobile/apple/OmnivoreKit/Sources/App/Views/WebReader/WebReaderCoordinator.swift); [WebReaderContainer.swift](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/mobile/apple/OmnivoreKit/Sources/App/Views/WebReader/WebReaderContainer.swift)). UIKit has the same two triggers as `hidesBarsOnSwipe` and `hidesBarsOnTap` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uinavigationcontroller/hidesbarsonswipe)). iOS 26 adds `tabBarMinimizeBehavior = .onScrollDown`, which "re-expands when scrolling in the opposite direction" *[Apple]* ([WWDC25 284](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/)). Apple's fluid-interfaces session says the hysteresis before a drag is recognized "is usually **10 points** in iOS" *[Apple]* ([WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)).

What makes the behavior calm rather than twitchy is the reveal threshold. Readwise Reader renders documents in a webview, at least on Android according to its own changelog. On 9 October 2026 it shipped "Made revealing the menus take a deliberate pull" and "a scroll ending no longer counts as a tap". A week later it made the tap that dismisses a highlight menu "do one thing only", instead of also bringing the toolbars back *[native]* ([Readwise changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)). Readwise does not publish the pull distance. The lesson is still clear: a reading thumb jitters, and a 10 pt reversal is not intent.

Paginated readers toggle chrome with a tap instead. Apple Books, Kindle, Kobo and Libby all use a tap on the center of the page *[native]* ([Libby Help](https://help.libbyapp.com/en-us/6063.htm); [Kobo Help](https://help.kobo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360017708194-Turn-pages-and-skip-chapters-in-your-book-on-the-Kobo-Books-app-iOS-Android)). Readest's code makes "center" precise *[code]*:
- The band from **37.5% to 62.5%** of the width toggles chrome.
- While the bars are visible, a tap anywhere else only hides them and does not turn the page.
- A swipe up toggles chrome when `deltaY < −10 px`, `|dy| > 2·|dx|` and `|dx|` is under 30% of the width.
- Tap slop is **15 px**, the double-tap window is 250 ms, and a long hold is 500 ms.
- The header fades over **300 ms**.

Sources: [usePagination.ts](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/app/reader/hooks/usePagination.ts); [useIframeEvents.ts](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/app/reader/hooks/useIframeEvents.ts); [constants.ts](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/services/constants.ts); [HeaderBar.tsx](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/app/reader/components/HeaderBar.tsx). The "first tap only hides" rule prevents the most common irritation: a page turn when the user only meant to dismiss the bars.

The status bar is part of the chrome. **Apple Books hides the status bar while you read and shows it when the reading menu opens**, and a "Show Status" setting keeps it visible *[native]* ([Gadget Hacks](https://ios.gadgethacks.com/how-to/20-big-new-features-and-changes-coming-apple-books-your-iphone-0385075)). Kindle's clock-visibility toggle and Readwise's July 2026 "Show clock" exist because a hidden status bar takes the time away *[native]* ([eBookFriendly](https://ebookfriendly.com/amazon-kindle-ios-app-revamped-menu-february-2020/); [Readwise Aug 2026 update](https://readwise.io/reader/update-aug2026)). The opposite approach failed in public. Apple Books' iOS 16 menu button was always visible and "can't be made to go away with a tap or slide" *[native]* ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2022/10/03/apples-books-ios-16/)). One critic estimated a **~50% hit rate** on it ([BasicAppleGuy](https://basicappleguy.com/basicappleblog/build-a-better-books)). The iOS 27.2 beta replaces it with a stock three-dot Liquid Glass menu ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1072973)).

Auto-scroll is the one exception to hiding. Dwell's September 2026 release made the menu bar "stay visible while using Read Along" *[native]* ([Dwell App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dwell-audio-bible/id1343917374)). Readwise's text-to-speech stops following the text "when it detects your scrolling" and swaps its ±15 s buttons for **Return** and **Jump**. Since October 2026 the controls live in "a floating player that shrinks into a pill as you scroll" *[native]* ([Readwise TTS FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/text-to-speech); [changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)). YouVersion's web reader is the deliberate counter-example. It never hides on scroll. Its toolbar is sticky, 56 px tall, 97% opaque with a 2 px backdrop blur. Instead it offers an explicit Focus Mode (key `f`). Focus Mode puts one attribute on the root, and CSS keyed on `:root:has([data-reader-focus-mode])` hides the rail, tab bar and footer. The floating arrows get `visibility:hidden` rather than `display:none`, so the layout does not shift *[web]* ([focus-mode JS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/36ribu1licvns.js); [main CSS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/120d2unqof6av.css)).

**Building it in Pager.** The top bar ([ReaderTopBar.tsx](../src/components/reader/ReaderTopBar.tsx)) and the tab bar ([BottomTabBar.tsx](../src/components/shell/BottomTabBar.tsx)) are React elements, so the core behavior is web-only. In the scroll view:
1. Record `scrollTop` at `touchstart`.
2. Hide the bars after 10 px of downward travel. One CSS px equals one pt at `initial-scale=1` *[inference]*.
3. Reveal them only after a deliberate upward pull, at the top of the content, or inside the end zone. Start the pull at 30–40 px and tune it on a device *[inference]*.
4. Animate with Substack's 250 ms ease-out-expo, and pad the content by the bar height, as Omnivore's `contentInset` does.

In paginated mode, copy Readest's center band and its "first tap only hides" rule. Every reader tap should pass through a guard that drops it if the scroll position or page changed during the touch, or if momentum ended within about 150 ms *[inference]*. The guard can use `scrollend` on iOS 26.2+ *[Apple]* ([MDN BCD](https://unpkg.com/@mdn/browser-compat-data/data.json)). Hiding the system status bar in step with the chrome **needs native help**: a bridge message that drives SwiftUI `.statusBarHidden()` and `.persistentSystemOverlays(.hidden)`. Pair it with an optional clock in [PageFooter.tsx](../src/components/reader/PageFooter.tsx).

## Page turns default to an instant cut, and motion is opt-in

The biggest surprise is how little the market leaders animate page turns. **Kindle's "Page Turn Animation" is off by default.** It is the last item under Aa › More. When Continuous Scrolling is on, the setting is disabled with the copy "Turn off Continuous Scroll to enable setting" *[native]* ([MakeUseOf, Aug 2026](https://www.makeuseof.com/kindle-page-turn-animation-setting/); [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/251718919)). That mutual exclusion suggests that the paged and scroll modes are separate renderers *[inference]*. Apple Books went the other way and was punished for it. iOS 16 replaced the curl with "a simple-looking swipe animation" that could not be changed ([Gadget Hacks](https://ios.gadgethacks.com/how-to/20-big-new-features-and-changes-coming-apple-books-your-iphone-0385075)). One critic called this "the most egregious and unnecessary killjoy change" ([BasicAppleGuy](https://basicappleguy.com/basicappleblog/build-a-better-books)). In iOS 16.4 Apple added a picker with Slide, Curl and None, with **Curl back as the default** *[native]* ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2023/02/28/ios-16-4-apple-books-page-turning-animation)). By iOS 18 the options were listed as "Curl, Fast Fade, or Scroll" ([Apple, iOS 18 guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/read-books-iphc1af7c57/18.0/ios/18.0)). The 16.4 "None" was a misnomer: a slight cross-fade remained, and it disappeared only when Reduce Motion was on *[native]* ([Gadget Hacks](https://ios.gadgethacks.com/how-to/get-page-turning-curl-animation-back-apple-books-for-iphone-and-ipad-0385329)). Kobo's 2025 rebuild offers **Curl, Swipe, Fade and Off**, plus a beta "pageless" mode that "acts as an infinite scroll" ([Good e-Reader](https://goodereader.com/blog/kobo-ereader-news/rakuten-kobo-redesigns-its-app-for-the-iphone)). Yomu offers "standard slide or fast fade" and a switch that turns off the screen-edge back gesture inside the reader *[native]* ([Yomu release notes](https://www.yomu-reader.com/release-notes/)). The consensus menu is therefore slide, fade and none, with curl as a nostalgia option.

Turning gestures follow two families. In Apple Books you tap the right margin or swipe right-to-left, and "Both Margins Advance" lets either margin go forward ([Apple, iOS 27 guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/iphone/read-books-iphc1af7c57/ios)). Swipe-down-to-close was "not available if vertical scrolling is on", because the vertical gesture belongs to the scroll view *[native]* ([Apple, iOS 26 guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/read-books-iphc1af7c57/26/ios/26)). Kobo adds the most physical gesture in the set: **hold the left or right edge and pages flip, faster the longer you hold** *[native]* ([Kobo Help](https://help.kobo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360017708194-Turn-pages-and-skip-chapters-in-your-book-on-the-Kobo-Books-app-iOS-Android)). Readwise Reader made the most instructive choice. It "uses vertical pagination rather than sideways swiping so that text highlighting and swipe gestures keep working", and turns pages with a margin tap. Its 2025 fixes read like a specification:
- "Long presses on the reading view will no longer trigger navigation".
- Highlighting near the page margin no longer turns the page.
- Users can "advance pages without accidentally creating highlights".

Sources: *[native]* ([Readwise Appearance FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/appearance); [changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)). Libby refuses continuous scroll for books on research grounds. OverDrive said in 2018 that it is "great for long form articles but it creates a challenging reading experience for books" *[native]* ([OverDrive](https://company.overdrive.com/2018/03/07/libby-update/)).

No public source gives tap-zone widths for Kindle or Apple Books. Kindle shows the cost of tall edge zones: footnote return links near the left edge get read as page turns *[native]* ([Straight Dope](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/kindle-footnotes-iphone-and-ipad/643734)). BookFusion went the other way, with a configurable **3×3 tap grid** whose cells can be Page Left, Page Right, Toggle Toolbar, Play/Pause or No Action *[native]* ([BookFusion blog](https://www.blog.bookfusion.com/bookfusion-ios-1-31-0-custom-tap-zones-other-improvements/)). The hard motion numbers come from open source *[code]*:
- The foliate-js paginator snaps in **300 ms `easeOutQuad`** and locks for **100 ms** between turns.
- On release it targets the viewport midpoint plus `velocity × pageSize`, so every 1 px/ms of flick adds one page ([paginator.js](https://github.com/johnfactotum/foliate-js/blob/main/paginator.js)).
- Readest defaults to "push", which slides the whole strip. Its "slide" and "curl" styles layer the outgoing page over the incoming one, using View Transitions, over **450 ms** ([types/book.ts](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/types/book.ts); [capturedTurn.ts](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/app/reader/utils/capturedTurn.ts)).

Native scroll physics are the yardstick that any custom gesture is judged against. Reverse engineering gives UIScrollView's deceleration as a per-millisecond decay of **0.998 (`.normal`) and 0.99 (`.fast`)** *[RE]* ([react-native-webview PR](https://github.com/react-native-webview/react-native-webview/pull/2815); [Lobanov](https://teletype.in/@swift-discipline/how-uiscrollview-works)). The rubber band is `(1 − 1/(x·0.55/d + 1))·d` *[RE]* ([gist](https://gist.github.com/originell/6961057)). On an 844 pt screen, a 100 pt pull therefore shows only about 52 pt of movement *[inference]*.

**Building it in Pager.** The paginated reader currently jumps with `el.scrollLeft = scrollLeftFor(...)` ([usePagination.ts](../src/components/reader/usePagination.ts)), so a page change has no glide and no momentum. The web-only fix is to let WebKit's own scroll view do the work. Put `scroll-snap-type: x mandatory; scroll-snap-stop: always` on the column scroller, with one snap point per page. A swipe then gets UIScrollView momentum and snapping for free. Keep Readium's WebKit rules: the column gap in px, the single-column width at 100% of the viewport, and the gap included in the page math ([Readium CSS](https://readium.org/css/docs/CSS03-injection_and_pagination.html)). Offer Slide, Fast Fade and None. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, use an instant cut, as Books' None does. When scroll mode is active, show Kindle's explanatory copy rather than hiding the option. Margin taps and Kobo's edge-hold accelerating flip are simple timers on pointer events. A real curl would need native snapshots in a `UIPageViewController(.pageCurl)` overlay. Skip it. One piece **needs native help**: [WebView.swift](../ios/Sources/Pager/WebView.swift) sets `allowsBackForwardNavigationGestures = true`, and a bridge message should turn that off while the paginated reader is open, so that edge swipes turn pages instead of navigating back.

## Chapter ends stay quiet while book ends ask one question

No app studied shows an interstitial at the end of a chapter. Every one of them uses the chapter boundary for calm continuity instead. YouVersion's web reader shows how carefully that can be done *[web]* ([chapter JS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/2jxxevxe-azgb.js); [main CSS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/120d2unqof6av.css); [reader CSS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/1kkxuxu-rkriu.css)):
- **The order at the end of a chapter** is the last verse, then a 14 px grey copyright line, then a 96 px gap, then related videos.
- **The previous and next chapter buttons** are **48×48 px with a 12 px radius**, filled `#edebeb` in light mode and `#353333` in dark mode. They sit 16 px from the screen edges and 16 px above the tab bar.
- **They float, then rest.** They live in a `position: sticky` container with `pointer-events: none`, placed after the chapter text. They therefore float over the text while you read and come to rest at the chapter's end without any scroll-direction logic.
- **The loading spinner appears only when needed.** If navigation takes longer than **150 ms**, a spinner fades in over 150 ms. A fast, cached load shows nothing.

See [youversion_web_reconstructed_reader_dark.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/youversion_web_reconstructed_reader_dark.png>). On Android, a "Continuous Scrolling" switch replaces the buttons with scrolling between chapters *[native]* ([YouVersion Help: Android](https://help.youversion.com/l/en/article/mya0fknmzo-bible-settings-android)). Dwell's Read mode scrolls straight into the next book *[native]* ([Dwell App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dwell-audio-bible/id1343917374)). In paginated EPUBs the craft is invisible. Readwise added a fade that "should now only appear on EPUBs near the end of chapters". In October 2026 it changed pagination so that "each page starts and ends cleanly", with no peeking previous page and no lines hidden behind the footer *[native]* ([Readwise changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)). Kobo users complain precisely when this fails: "the last few words at the end of a chapter would drop off" *[native]* ([Kobo App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kobo-books/id301259483)).

At the end of an article, the read-later apps show the actions and leave the decision to the reader. Omnivore's bottom toolbar (Archive, Move to Inbox, Share, Delete) appears on its own once the reader is **within 140 pt of the end**. Archiving pops the reader and then shows an "Archived" snackbar with **Undo for 2000 ms** *[code]* ([WebReaderCoordinator.swift](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/mobile/apple/OmnivoreKit/Sources/App/Views/WebReader/WebReaderCoordinator.swift); [WebReaderContainer.swift](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/mobile/apple/OmnivoreKit/Sources/App/Views/WebReader/WebReaderContainer.swift)). Readwise returns you to the list by default, with an opt-in "auto-advance" to the next document. Since August 2026 its Modern toolbar lets you swipe between documents on the toolbar, not on the text, which keeps the gesture away from text selection *[native]* ([Readwise Navigation FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/navigation); [Aug 2026 update](https://readwise.io/reader/update-aug2026)). Instapaper and Readwise both offer shake-to-undo *[native]* ([9to5Mac 2023](https://9to5mac.com/2023/07/28/instapaper-drag-and-drop-shortcuts/)). None of them archives automatically at the bottom of a document. A quick flick reaches the end without the reader having read anything *[inference]*.

Newsletter platforms treat the end as a conversion surface. Substack ends a post with a subscribe widget, the action row, comments, a "Previous" link, Top/Latest/Discussions tabs and an orange "Ready for more?" band. There is no pull-to-next gesture *[web]* ([Substack web reader](https://on.substack.com/p/the-tribe-has-spoken-how-drop-your); [substack_web_end_region.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/substack_web_end_region.png>)). Its like heart scales an overlay from 0 over **500 ms ease-out-expo**, with a `pop` keyframe to 1.5× and a spark burst *[web]*. Medium ends with "Written by" and "Published in" cards and recommendations, each with a "show less" control ([medium_web_recommended_dark.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/medium_web_recommended_dark.png>)). Member-only stories fade their last lines into the background before "Create an account to read the full story." *[web]* ([medium_web_paywall_fade_dark.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/medium_web_paywall_fade_dark.png>)). The clap allows **up to 50 per story**, and you add more by holding the button *[native]* ([Medium Help: claps](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/115011350967-About-claps)). Community recreations of the clap use about a 300 ms repeat interval, but that is third-party code, not Medium's ([MediumClap-Android](https://github.com/wajahatkarim3/MediumClap-Android/)). Medium also throws a full-screen sign-up overlay after about 400 px on free stories ([medium_web_signup_overlay.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/medium_web_signup_overlay.png>)). It is a clear example of what a personal reader should never do.

At the end of a book, the apps ask one question: is this finished? They differ in how gently they ask. Kindle's "Book End Actions" (2013), called "Before You Go" on e-readers, fires at a publisher-set end marker or when you swipe past the last page. It has no off switch, and it misfires at the end of each book inside a box set *[native]* ([iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/805352/Why+does+the+%E2%80%9CBefore+You+Go%E2%80%9D+screen+pop+up+part+way+through+a+book); [KBoards](https://www.kboards.com/threads/rate-book-popup-appearing-in-random-places-on-kindle.244699/)). Libby asks outside the reader. Once `journey.progress >= .95`, its Shelf shows "**Finished?**", which expands into "Return Early". The copy is unusually considerate: "Not finished yet? Nobody is waiting, so you may renew your loan instead." *[code]* ([Libby main.js](https://libbyapp.com/dewey-22.2.0/src/main.js); [strings](https://libbyapp.com/dewey-22.2.0/inc/str/en-US.js)). Apple Books folds finishing into Reading Goals. The defaults are **5 minutes a day and 3 books a year**, and finished covers fill "Books Read This Year" *[native]* ([Apple Reading Goals](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/iphone/set-reading-goals-iph6013e96f4/ios)). Omnivore adds a small courtesy: an item reopened at **≥98% progress starts from the top**, because it is a re-read *[code]* ([Article.tsx](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/packages/web/components/templates/article/Article.tsx)).

**Building it in Pager.** All of this is web-only except one haptic:
- **Section buttons in scroll mode.** Put YouVersion's sticky previous/next pair after each section. Size them at 48 px, inset them 16 px, and show the spinner only after 150 ms (`transition-delay`).
- **Clean page edges in paginated mode.** Check that the last line of a spine item never sits under [PageFooter.tsx](../src/components/reader/PageFooter.tsx).
- **An end card at the end of the book.** Show it within about 140 pt of the end in scroll mode, or on the last page. It holds "Finished" with a date, "Mark as finished", an annotation count that links to the notes list, and "Back to library". It never pops up mid-book.
- **A "Mark as finished?" suggestion in the library** once progress reaches 95% or more.
- **A 2 s undo snackbar** after any state change.
- **Continuous scrolling across spine items** as an option, by lazy-mounting neighbors with IntersectionObserver.
- **The ≥98% re-read rule.**
- **One success haptic** when the book is finished. This part **needs native help**.

## Progress turns into navigation once you can tap it

The best progress indicators are controls, not labels. **Kindle's footer is a single tap target that cycles** through page in book, time left in chapter, time left in book, location, and off *[native]* ([Just Kindle Books](https://www.justkindlebooks.com/article_jkb/how-to-view-your-kindle-reading-progress/)). Its time estimates come from a learned reading speed. Until the app has enough data it says so honestly with "Learning reading speed"; a 2023 bug left that text stuck until version 6.74 *[native]* ([Amazon forum](https://www.amazonforum.com/s/question/0D54P00008CSz7ESAT/recalculate-or-reset-time-left-in-chapter-ios-app)). In August 2024 Kindle changed the e-reader version of this indicator. It became smaller and aligned to the book's margins, and users said it was hard to read, lagged behind taps and needed a precise touch *[native, e-reader]* ([The eBook Reader](https://blog.the-ebook-reader.com/2024/08/09/last-kindle-update-changed-reading-progress-indicator-and-some-hate-it/)). That backlash gives three rules: keep the text legible, switch instantly, and make the hit area much larger than the text. Readwise offers the same five formats (percent, page, time left in chapter, time left in book, hidden) plus a clock *[native]* ([Readwise changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)). Readest's footer copy is a good template *[code]*: "{{time}} left in chapter", "{{time}} and {{count}} pages left in chapter" and "{{count}} pages left in book". The footer is a three-zone row: progress, remaining, and clock with battery ([ProgressBar.tsx](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/app/reader/components/ProgressBar.tsx)). For the estimate itself, Medium documents its formula: **265 words per minute** with an adjustment for images, and **500 characters per minute** for Chinese, Japanese and Korean *[native]* ([Medium Help: Read time](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/214991667-Read-time)).

Libby has the most inventive progress UI. It turns the bottom chrome into a navigator *[native]* ([Libby Help 6045](https://help.libbyapp.com/en-us/6045.htm); [Scenic Regional guide, Aug 2025](https://scenicregional.org/wp-content/uploads/LibbyMobileAppHelpGuide_August2025.pdf)):
- A circular book-cover thumbnail sits centered on a hairline as the "knob", flanked by "pg. 14" and "of 613".
- Below it, "◂ 1p" and "5p ▸" flank the chapter title, with "TABLE OF CONTENTS" in small caps. The chapter-skip buttons are labeled with the distance to the chapter's start and end, so they double as a "pages left in chapter" readout.
- At the bottom, a ruler-style timeline shows chapter breaks, bookmarks and highlights as heavier ticks.
- Tapping the page number shows the percentage.

The labels in the screenshot put about **9.3 px per page**, with page 14 at the center. That means the ruler scrolls under a fixed knob like a tape measure, rather than a thumb moving along a fixed track *[inference]*. Kindle's Page Flip (2016) is the other navigator idea. It pins your current page to the side of the screen while you browse, lets you zoom out to a bird's-eye view, and renders thumbnails that reflect your font and margins *[native]* ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/06/28/amazon-introduces-page-flip-for-kindle/)).

Apple Books shows what happens when progress is hidden. In iOS 16–26, pages left in the chapter appeared only when the menu was open or when you tapped the page number. The scrubber was a hidden gesture: touch and hold "Contents", then drag. A critic described that control as one that "looks like a button, but functions like a slider" *[native]* ([Gadget Hacks](https://ios.gadgethacks.com/how-to/20-big-new-features-and-changes-coming-apple-books-your-iphone-0385075); [BasicAppleGuy](https://basicappleguy.com/basicappleblog/build-a-better-books)). iOS 27 replaced it with "the slider at the bottom of the page", and 27.2 beta adds visible "progress bars" *[native]* ([Apple, iOS 27 guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/iphone/read-books-iphc1af7c57/ios); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1075324)). In scroll views, Medium's 2026 table of contents works as a progress indicator. Its popover marks the current H2 with `aria-current="location"` and fades in over 180 ms *[web]* ([Medium web reader](https://medium.com/blog/dark-mode-for-medium-is-finally-here-002977326982)).

**Building it in Pager.** All of this is web-only except the haptic:
- **Footer cycling.** Turn the text in [PageFooter.tsx](../src/components/reader/PageFooter.tsx) into one tap target of at least 44 pt that cycles page / time left in chapter / time left in book / percent / off, switches instantly, and persists the choice.
- **Time estimates.** Compute time left from a rolling words-per-minute figure that ignores idle gaps and fast skims. Show "Learning reading speed" until there are enough samples.
- **Top progress line.** Keep the 2 px accent line already in [ReaderTopBar.tsx](../src/components/reader/ReaderTopBar.tsx), which animates its width over 200 ms.
- **Scrubber.** Show a preview label with the chapter title and page while dragging, and commit on release. Add Libby's distance-labeled chapter buttons ("‹ 3p", "12p ›"), and draw bookmarks and highlights as ticks.
- **TOC drawer.** Open [TocDrawer.tsx](../src/components/reader/TocDrawer.tsx) on the current section.
- **Chapter haptic.** A selection haptic when the scrubber crosses a chapter boundary **needs native help**.

## Every jump leaves a way back

Every serious reader now offers a return control after a non-linear jump. The designs differ, and some are clearly better than others *[native unless noted]*:

| App | Return control |
|---|---|
| Apple Books, iOS 16–26 | "Tiny numbers with arrows" at top-left and top-right ([Gadget Hacks](https://ios.gadgethacks.com/how-to/20-big-new-features-and-changes-coming-apple-books-your-iphone-0385075)) |
| Apple Books, iOS 27 | An "Undo Page Skip" button at bottom-left. Tapping it again returns you, so it is a two-state toggle, not a history ([Apple, iOS 27 guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/iphone/read-books-iphc1af7c57/ios)) |
| Apple Books, iOS 27.2 beta | The back control is larger and sits lower-left ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1072973)) |
| Kobo | The arrow "only appears after you've skipped a chapter or go to a different page using the slider" ([Kobo Help](https://help.kobo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360017708194-Turn-pages-and-skip-chapters-in-your-book-on-the-Kobo-Books-app-iOS-Android)) |
| Libby | A "Recent place, just now" button plus a Recent Places list, "erased each time you close a title" ([Libby Help 6285](https://help.libbyapp.com/en-us/6285.htm)) |
| Readest *[code]* | Go Back and Go Forward buttons in the progress panel ([NavigationPanel.tsx](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/app/reader/components/footerbar/NavigationPanel.tsx)) |
| Kindle | Small black dots on the scrubber mark previous locations. They can hide behind the knob, and VoiceOver users report no back button at all, so they bookmark before following a link ([KBoards](https://www.kboards.com/threads/question-about-going-back-in-kindle-for-android.196587/); [AppleVis](https://applevis.com/forum/ios-ipados/kindle-app-navigation)) |

Kobo's conditional arrow is the cleanest of these: the control exists only when it is useful.

Footnotes are the most frequent jump, and the better answer is not to jump at all. YouVersion's web footnote opens as a popover in place *[web]* ([reader CSS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/1kkxuxu-rkriu.css)):
- The marker is a 24 px icon at 60% opacity.
- The popover is `width: calc(100vw - 2.5rem)`, capped at **460 px wide** and **60vh tall**, and scrolls internally.
- Text is 14/21 px, with a scale-and-fade reveal.

Kindle's pop-up footnotes close with an X or a tap outside *[native]* ([eathealthy365](https://eathealthy365.com/how-footnotes-and-endnotes-actually-work-on-your-kindle/)). The arrival needs care too. When Substack deep-links to a heading, it washes the heading in 20% of the accent color with an 8 px halo. The wash holds for 2 s and fades over 1 s: `animation: 3s ease-out forwards highlight-fade`, holding for the first 66% *[web]* ([Substack web reader](https://on.substack.com/p/the-tribe-has-spoken-how-drop-your)). That flash tells the eye where it landed. YouVersion applies a related rule to selection: if the tapped verse is within **350 px of the bottom of the viewport**, it smooth-scrolls the verse to the center so the action sheet does not cover it *[web]* ([selection JS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/291mtgegayhux.js)).

Position memory across sessions is where users are least forgiving. Kobo reviews complain about "not retaining the reader's place in the book" *[native]* ([Kobo App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kobo-books/id301259483)). A YouVersion review says "The app doesn't seem to stick to what you read last time" *[native]* ([YouVersion App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bible/id282935706)). Kindle keeps both a furthest page and a last page. Its re-reading users get thrown to a furthest page from years earlier and must clear it on a website *[native]* ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/359117/how-to-clear-your-furthest-read-page-on-kindle/)). Readwise shipped "return to your farthest read point" in October 2024. Its changelog also records bugs where long documents and EPUBs had their progress "set to 100%" on open *[native]* ([Readwise changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)). That bug is typical of sampling progress before layout has settled *[inference]*. Omnivore's recipe avoids it *[code]* ([Article.tsx](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/packages/web/components/templates/article/Article.tsx); [WebReaderContainer.swift](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/mobile/apple/OmnivoreKit/Sources/App/Views/WebReader/WebReaderContainer.swift)):
- Sample the scroll position every **2.5 s**.
- Save a percentage together with a paragraph anchor index.
- Restore with an *instant* jump to the anchor minus **100 px** of context.
- Let a deep link to a highlight take priority over the saved position.
- Mark any opened item at least 0.1% read.

**Building it in Pager.** All of this is web-only:
- **A session stack of places.** Push a location on every non-linear jump: the TOC drawer, search hits, footnotes, a scrubber release, and the annotation list.
- **A back pill.** Show a bottom-left "Back to p. 42" pill (chapter-relative in scroll mode). It flips to "Return to p. 87" after use and clears after a few page turns away from the jump target.
- **Footnote popovers.** Intercept `epub:type="noteref"` and same-document anchor links, and show the note in a popover styled like [AnnotationPopover.tsx](../src/components/reader/AnnotationPopover.tsx). Link hit-testing must take priority over the edge tap zones.
- **A landing flash.** Mark the landing paragraph with the 3 s highlight fade.
- **Restore rules.** [useReadingProgress.ts](../src/components/reader/useReadingProgress.ts) already saves an anchor-based position with a 1 s debounce and flushes it on `pagehide` and `visibilitychange`. Add three rules: keep separate "furthest" and "last" positions, apply the ≥98% re-read rule, and ignore progress events until the restore jump and `document.fonts.ready` have both completed.

A native nudge on `scenePhase == .background` that calls `evaluateJavaScript` to flush would be a belt-and-braces addition. It is optional.

## One tap highlights in the color you used last

The strongest convergence in the whole study is **recency-ordered highlight color**:
- **YouVersion iOS** shows "the three most recently used" of its five colors first. Tapping the current color shows an X that removes the highlight *[native]* ([YouVersion Help: iOS Highlights](https://help.youversion.com/l/en/article/ie1gz0nsr7-highlights-ios)).
- **YouVersion's web swatch strip** uses a **44 px pitch**. It moves the chosen color to the front and smooth-scrolls back to the start *[web]* ([selection JS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/291mtgegayhux.js)).
- **Kindle** offers yellow, blue, pink and orange, and the last color you used becomes the color of the next highlight *[native, third-party guide]* ([Kindle Export](https://www.kindleexport.com/blog/how-to-highlight-on-kindle)).
- **Libby** says "Tap **Highlight** to use the most recent highlight color, or pick a different color next to Highlight" *[native]* ([Libby Help 6136](https://help.libbyapp.com/en-us/6136.htm)).
- **Apple Books** puts Highlight, Add Note and Translate first in its menu, with Translate, Search, Copy and Share behind a forward arrow. Highlight offers color swatches and an underline. Removing a highlight that has a note asks "Remove Note" or "Remove Highlight & Note" *[native]* ([Apple: Annotate books](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/annotate-books-iph17bf340c1/27/ios/27)).
- **Readwise** drops color entirely. "Selected text becomes a highlight immediately" on release (with a toggle). A double-tap highlights a whole paragraph. Handles resize and merge highlights, and an annotation bar appears after creation *[native]* ([Readwise Highlights FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/highlights-tags-notes); [changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)).
- **Matter** uses a double-tap to highlight a sentence and a double-tap on a highlight to add a note *[native]* ([MacPowerUsers](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/matter-launches-version-2-0-update-to-the-read-it-later-app/29110)).

The motion around a selection is where the web readers give exact numbers. **YouVersion marks a selected verse with a 1 px dotted underline** in `#777a7b` (`#aaa` in dark mode). Its action sheet slides from `translateY(calc(100% + 24px))` to 0 in **150 ms `cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1)`**. While hidden the sheet is `inert`, and the transition turns off under reduced motion. A highlight's background then fades in over **750 ms `cubic-bezier(.42,1,.16,.93)`**, a soft "ink soaking in" feel. The text color flips to white or black using the W3C brightness (≥125) and color-difference (≥500) formula *[web]* ([selection JS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/291mtgegayhux.js); [reader CSS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/1kkxuxu-rkriu.css)). See [youversion_web_reconstructed_verse_selected_sheet.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/youversion_web_reconstructed_verse_selected_sheet.png>) and [youversion_web_reconstructed_highlight_v1.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/youversion_web_reconstructed_highlight_v1.png>). Substack's custom selection menu enters in **140 ms `cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)`**, from transparent and 4 px higher, with no animation under reduced motion *[web]*. Medium paints highlights as a soft `#E8F3E8` `<mark>` and lets readers "Hide highlights" per story *[web; native]* ([Medium Help: highlights](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/214406358-About-highlights)).

Gesture timing comes from open source, and it shows how to keep selection from fighting page turns *[code]*. Readest's "instant highlight" engages only after the finger rests **300 ms with under 10 px of movement**, so taps and swipes still turn pages ([useTextSelector.ts](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/app/reader/hooks/useTextSelector.ts)). A selection dragged into the bottom-right or top-left corner turns the page after a **500 ms dwell**. The corner zone is a quarter-ellipse of 15% of each axis, capped at 50 px. A bar grows along the armed edge "so holding there reads as 'keep holding to turn'" ([useAutoPageTurn.ts](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/app/reader/hooks/useAutoPageTurn.ts)). Omnivore separates single and double taps with a 250 ms timer, so every single tap is delayed by 250 ms ([HighlightsLayer.tsx](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/packages/web/components/templates/article/HighlightsLayer.tsx)). That is the price of adding double-tap-to-highlight on top of tap-to-toggle-chrome.

WKWebView sets the technical boundaries:
- **The edit menu.** Since iOS 18.2, a view controller's `buildMenu(with:)` override is no longer called when a WKWebView is first responder. The working fix is to **subclass WKWebView, override `buildMenu(with:)`, call `super`, then add or remove items** *[Apple forums]* ([thread 770127](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/770127)).
- **Menu styling.** Readium confirms that you "cannot customize the style of the native iOS text selection menu". A fully custom menu means suppressing the native one and positioning your own from the selection frame ([Readium #385](https://github.com/readium/swift-toolkit/discussions/385)).
- **Selection styling.** `::selection` is not supported on iOS. The CSS Custom Highlight API is available from 17.2, but it is ignored on `user-select: none` content *[Apple/BCD]* ([MDN BCD](https://unpkg.com/@mdn/browser-compat-data/data.json)).
- **Touch handlers.** Safari 27 fixed "touch-handle text selection blocked by JavaScript touch handling". Never call `preventDefault()` on touches over reading text ([WebKit: Safari 27](https://webkit.org/blog/17967/)).
- **Omnivore's hybrid.** JS posts the tapped highlight's rect to Swift so the app can show a native menu. In a plain browser, the web highlight bar docks at the bottom on touch devices so it never collides with the system callout *[code]* ([HighlightsLayer.tsx](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/packages/web/components/templates/article/HighlightsLayer.tsx)).

**Building it in Pager.** Pager tracks selection with `selectionchange` ([useSelection.ts](../src/tools/useSelection.ts)), draws its own [SelectionMenu.tsx](../src/components/reader/SelectionMenu.tsx), and already paints highlights with the Custom Highlight API ([useHighlightPainting.ts](../src/components/reader/useHighlightPainting.ts)). Pager has to choose between two routes, and both **need native help**. The first is a `PagerWebView: WKWebView` subclass that inserts Highlight (last color), Note, Copy and Define into the native menu, with each action calling JS. The second keeps the web SelectionMenu and suppresses the system menu in the same subclass. The native route keeps the loupe, handles, Look Up and Translate, and avoids two menus appearing at once, so it is the better default. These parts are web-only:
- **Last color by default.** A one-tap highlight uses the last color, and a recency-ordered swatch strip sits beside it.
- **Tapping an existing highlight** opens AnnotationPopover, with Apple's "Remove Note / Remove Highlight & Note" choice.
- **Keep the selection visible.** Apply YouVersion's 350 px rule whenever a popover would cover the selection.
- **Wait for a stable selection.** If Pager keeps any web selection UI, show it only after the selection has been stable for about 150–250 ms *[inference]*.

Whether a `::highlight()` background can run YouVersion's 750 ms fade is unverified. Test it on a device, and fall back to a short-lived overlay if it cannot *[inference]*. A soft impact haptic when a highlight is committed **needs native help**.

## Settings reflow live behind a half-height panel without losing your place

The settings panels share a structure. Size is changed in discrete steps, themes bundle several properties, there is a light/auto/dark switch, and brightness sits close at hand. The panel lives over the live page:
- **Apple Books** has a Themes & Settings sheet with A−/A+ buttons and a page-turn picker. It has a Light/Dark/Match Device/Match Surroundings control and a brightness bar (removed in iOS 16 and back by iOS 17). Its six themes are **Original, Quiet, Paper, Bold, Calm, Focus**. Each remembers its own customizations, and "Reset Theme" undoes them. A Customize switch unlocks line, character and word spacing, margins (iOS 18+) and justification *[native]* ([Apple, iOS 27 guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/iphone/read-books-iphc1af7c57/ios); [Gadget Hacks](https://ios.gadgethacks.com/how-to/20-big-new-features-and-changes-coming-apple-books-your-iphone-0385075)).
- **Kindle's** Aa panel has three tabs. **Font** opens by default and has 14 marked size levels. **Layout** has spacing, alignment, page color, "Update Page Using System Theme", columns and Continuous Scrolling. **More** has the clock, page animation and Reading Ruler. **Brightness appears on every tab** *[native]* ([eBookFriendly](https://ebookfriendly.com/amazon-kindle-ios-app-revamped-menu-february-2020/); [Pocket-lint](https://www.pocket-lint.com/kindle-app-secret-features/)).
- **Readwise** defaults to **20 px text (range 14–80) and 1.4 line spacing**. Its six themes (Crisp, Sepia, Soft, Slate, Ink, Night) each have light and dark variants. A Light/Auto/Dark icon sits in the sheet's corner, and since February 2026 "tapping outside the appearance sheet … closes it" *[native]* ([Readwise Appearance FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/appearance); [changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)).
- **Libby** has a Text Scale slider with "Include accessibility sizes" and Lighting (Bright, Sepia, Dark) that follows the device by default *[native]* ([Libby Help 6046](https://help.libbyapp.com/en-us/6046.htm)).
- **Instapaper 10** narrowed its fonts to Literata, New York, Open Sans and Atkinson Hyperlegible ([9to5Mac, Jul 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/28/instapaper-update-adds-liquid-glass-home-screen-widgets-new-fonts-more/)).
- **Yomu** advertises "no unnecessary preferences" and lets you save any setting "as your personal default for each book" *[native]* ([yomu-reader.com](https://www.yomu-reader.com/)).
- **Kobo** has the most direct control: **pinch anywhere on the page to change font size**, with a size menu popping up *[native]* ([Kobo Help](https://help.kobo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360017708154-Adjust-font-size-and-font-type-on-the-Kobo-Books-app-iOS-Android)).

The web readers supply exact typography *[web]*. **YouVersion's three size presets render the serif at 16, 20 and 24 px, and each preset widens the column: 550, 600 and 700 px.** The measure therefore stays near 60–70 characters on iPad. Line height is 1.8. Verse numbers are raised with `position: relative; top: -.6em`, so they never disturb line spacing ([settings JS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/0r8tr9-rce__k.js); [reader CSS](https://www.bible.com/_next/static/chunks/1kkxuxu-rkriu.css); [youversion_web_reconstructed_size_large_24px.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/youversion_web_reconstructed_size_large_24px.png>)). On a 390 px phone, Substack and Medium converge on an **18 px serif body**:

| | Substack | Medium |
|---|---|---|
| Body type | Lora 18 px, line-height 1.6 | source-serif-pro 18/28 px |
| Gutters | 16 px | 24 px |
| Characters per line | about 40 | about 33 |
| Title | 32 px | 32 px |
| Captions | 14–15 px grey | 14–15 px grey |

Sources: [Substack](https://on.substack.com/p/the-tribe-has-spoken-how-drop-your); [Medium](https://medium.com/blog/dark-mode-for-medium-is-finally-here-002977326982); [substack_web_top_390.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/substack_web_top_390.png>); [medium_web_top_light_390.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/medium_web_top_light_390.png>). Medium's October 2026 dark mode uses a near-black `#171717` background with `#E5E5E5` text, not pure black and white *[web]* ([medium_web_top_dark_390.png](<../research_notes/iOS reading app UI patterns/screenshots/medium_web_top_dark_390.png>)).

Readest's code supplies the panel physics *[code]* ([MobileFooterBar.tsx](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/app/reader/components/footerbar/MobileFooterBar.tsx); [useSwipeToDismiss.ts](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/hooks/useSwipeToDismiss.ts); [Dialog.tsx](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/components/Dialog.tsx)):
- Settings open as **non-modal panels** floating `64px + 0.33·safeAreaBottom` above the bottom edge, so the page stays visible while sliders move.
- Sheets dismiss on a swipe with **velocity > 0.5 or more than 50% travel**.
- The exit takes `0.15 / max(v, 0.5)` s with ease-out and fires a **light impact haptic**.
- A sheet that is not dismissed springs back in 0.3 s.

The native alternative is a SwiftUI sheet with `presentationDetents([.medium, .large])`. It gets Apple's detent spring and glass for free *[Apple]* ([Apple: presentationDetents](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/presentationdetents(_:))).

No native app documents how it keeps your place when text reflows. Apple Books draws complaints that it "opens on the wrong page" *[native]* ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2022/10/03/apples-books-ios-16/)). YouVersion's web reader contains no anchoring code at all *[web]*. CSS `overflow-anchor` reached WebKit only in Safari 27, above Pager's iOS 17.5 floor ([WebKit: Safari 27](https://webkit.org/blog/17967/)). Readwise's 2026 fixes name the two hard parts of CSS pagination: "Text now stays put once a page is laid out", meaning font loading and image sizing no longer shift it, and "each page starts and ends cleanly" *[native]* ([Readwise changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)).

**Building it in Pager.** These parts are web-only:
- **A non-modal panel.** Make [ReadingSettings.tsx](../src/components/reader/ReadingSettings.tsx) a non-modal bottom panel over the live text that closes on an outside tap.
- **Named presets.** Offer three to six presets that bundle font, size, spacing and theme, with per-preset memory and Reset.
- **Stepped size.** Use a stepped size control, with column width tied to size on iPad.
- **Light/Auto/Dark.** Drive it from `prefers-color-scheme`.
- **Anchored reflow.** Before any change, record the first visible text node and its offset. Re-paginate after `document.fonts.ready`, with image boxes reserved. Then jump to the page that holds the anchor.

These parts **need native help**:
- **A brightness slider**, which needs `UIScreen.brightness`.
- **A selection haptic on each size step.**
- **Theme-colored web view backgrounds.** Set the WKWebView's `backgroundColor` and `underPageBackgroundColor` to match the theme, so there is no white flash.

Kobo's pinch-to-resize is web-only (WebKit `gesturestart`), but it must keep the same anchor.

## Haptics are undocumented everywhere, so Libby's 75 ms bridge is the model

**No source documents where YouVersion, Kindle, Apple Books, Substack or Medium fire haptics.** The only real implementations found are in code, and they are restrained. Libby's public bundle has a haptics module with `prepare()`, `select()`, `impact("light")` and `notify("success")`. Each sends `{name: "haptic:" + kind, dest: "shell"}` to the native shell. Calls are coalesced with **`GAP_MS = 75`**: a haptic within 75 ms of the previous one is deferred, not stacked. `select()` fires on chips, filters, map markers and carousel dots, and an in-app "Reduce Vibrations" setting turns everything off *[code]* ([Libby main.js](https://libbyapp.com/dewey-22.2.0/src/main.js); [strings](https://libbyapp.com/dewey-22.2.0/inc/str/en-US.js)). Readest's only haptic is a light impact when a sheet is swiped away *[code]* ([Dialog.tsx](https://github.com/readest/readest/blob/main/apps/readest-app/src/components/Dialog.tsx)). Readwise added an "option to disable haptics" in March 2023, and Instapaper's haptics "can be disabled in settings" *[native]* ([Readwise changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog); [9to5Mac 2023](https://9to5mac.com/2023/07/28/instapaper-drag-and-drop-shortcuts/)). Apple's guidance matches this restraint. Haptics should be consistent, sparing, optional and matched to the animation, because "the best haptic experience is one that people may not be conscious of, but miss when it's turned off" *[Apple]* ([HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)).

The web platform cannot do this alone. `navigator.vibrate` is not supported on iOS Safari or in the iOS WebView ([MDN BCD](https://unpkg.com/@mdn/browser-compat-data/data.json)). The `<input switch>` trick, which played the switch control's haptic, reportedly stopped working in iOS 26.5 ([ios-haptics README](https://unpkg.com/ios-haptics@3.1.1/README.md)). The bridge is cheap. A WKWebView message round trip measured **0.63 ms** on an iPad Air 2 with iOS 9, so the hop is not perceptible ([persistent.info](https://blog.persistent.info/2015/08/wkwebview-communication-latency.html)). The bigger cost is an unprepared Taptic Engine. `prepare()` keeps it ready for "typically seconds", so the bridge should prepare on touch-down and fire on commit *[Apple]* ([Apple: prepare()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uifeedbackgenerator/prepare())).

| Reading moment | Generator | Rationale *[inference from HIG meanings]* |
|---|---|---|
| Size or spacing step, theme segment, scrubber crossing a chapter | `UISelectionFeedbackGenerator` | "values are changing" |
| Pull-to-bookmark threshold crossed, sheet snapping to a detent, optional page-turn commit | `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator(.light)` or low-intensity `.rigid` | "snaps into place" |
| Highlight created | `.soft` or `.light` impact | a mild "placed" feel |
| Book finished, note saved | `UINotificationFeedbackGenerator.success` | use rarely; not on every chapter |
| Sync failure | `.error` | |
| First or last page reached | none | the rubber band is the feedback |

**Building it in Pager.** This **needs native help**. [WebView.swift](../ios/Sources/Pager/WebView.swift) registers no message handlers today. Add a `pager.haptic` `WKScriptMessageHandler` that accepts `{t: "prepare", kind}` on `pointerdown` and `{t: "fire", kind}` on commit, backed by long-lived generators. In JS, mirror Libby's 75 ms coalescing and gate everything behind a "Haptics" setting.

## Motion lives between 80 and 750 ms, mostly ease-out

Laid side by side, the numbers form a clear vocabulary. Taps and menus take 80–150 ms. Bars, page snaps and sheets take 250–300 ms. Layered page turns, highlight "ink" and attention flashes take 450 ms to 3 s. Nobody uses symmetric ease-in-out for UI that enters. The curves are ease-out: ease-out-expo `(.19,1,.22,1)`, `(.16,1,.3,1)`, Tailwind's default `(.4,0,.2,1)`, or `easeOutQuad`. Substack's design tokens summarize the family: **125, 250 and 500 ms**, a drawer curve of `cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)` that resembles an iOS sheet, and a bounce curve `cubic-bezier(.15,1,.75,1.2)` *[web]* ([Substack web reader](https://on.substack.com/p/the-tribe-has-spoken-how-drop-your)).

For anything the user can grab, springs beat fixed curves. Apple's model has two parameters: a perceptual duration T and a bounce b, with mass fixed at 1, so stiffness = (2π/T)² and damping ratio ζ = 1 − b. Apple's worked example, `Spring(duration: 0.5, bounce: 0.3)`, gives stiffness **157.9** and damping **17.6** *[Apple]* ([Apple: Spring](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/spring)). WWDC23 warns that a bounce of 15% "doesn't feel very bouncy yet", 30% is noticeable, and values above about 0.4 call for caution ([WWDC23 10158](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/)). WWDC18 advises starting at 100% damping and adding overshoot only when the gesture has momentum. Music's swipe-dismiss uses 80% damping ([WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)). Motion's source maps bounce "as SwiftUI does", with `visualDuration = T/1.2`. react-spring matches exactly with `{frequency: T, damping: ζ}` ([Motion spring.ts](https://github.com/motiondivision/motion/blob/main/packages/motion-dom/src/animation/generators/spring.ts); [react-spring AnimationConfig.ts](https://github.com/pmndrs/react-spring/blob/main/packages/core/src/AnimationConfig.ts)).

| Apple spring *[Apple]* | Stiffness | Damping | Overshoot | Settles to 0.1% *[inference]* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `.smooth` (T 0.5, b 0) | 157.9 | 25.13 | 0% | 734 ms |
| `.snappy` (T 0.5, b 0.15) | 157.9 | 21.36 | 0.6% | 696 ms |
| `.bouncy` (T 0.5, b 0.3) | 157.9 | 17.59 | 4.6% | 818 ms |
| legacy `.spring()` (0.5, ζ 0.825) | 157.9 | 20.73 | 1.0% | 688 ms |
| `interactiveSpring` (0.15, ζ 0.86) | 1754.6 | 72.05 | 0.5% | 209 ms |

Sources: [snappy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation/snappy(duration:extrabounce:)); [legacy spring](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation/spring(response:dampingfraction:blendduration:)); [interactiveSpring](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation/interactivespring(response:dampingfraction:blendduration:)).

For fire-and-forget transitions, CSS `linear()` (Safari 17.2+) can carry a sampled spring ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/css-linear-easing-function)). Sampling `.smooth` gives a 734 ms curve: `linear(0, 0.018 2.2%, 0.062 4.4%, 0.123 6.5%, 0.411 15.2%, 0.478 17.4%, 0.54 19.6%, 0.597 21.8%, 0.648 24%, 0.694 26.1%, 0.735 28.3%, 0.771 30.5%, 0.831 34.9%, 0.876 39.2%, 0.91 43.6%, 0.945 50.1%, 0.976 61%, 1)` *[inference]*. Note that the CSS duration is the settle time (about 700–820 ms), not T, and most of the movement happens in the first 40%. CSS transitions restart from the current value with zero velocity, so a drawer reversed mid-flight "kinks". The TOC drawer, the settings sheet and the chrome reveal therefore need a JS spring that carries the release velocity. For flicks, project the end point with `x + (v/1000)·0.998/(1 − 0.998)` and spring to the nearest detent *[RE]* ([WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/); [Lobanov](https://teletype.in/@swift-discipline/how-uiscrollview-works)).

WKWebView has one hard ceiling. **`requestAnimationFrame` inside WKWebView is capped at 60 Hz on ProMotion iPhones.** WebKit bug 294338 was still open in September 2026. `CADisableMinimumFrameDurationOnPhone` does not lift the cap, and the only known unlock is a private preference ([WebKit bug 294338](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=294338)). The practical rule is to move as much motion as possible into native scrolling (UIScrollView runs in the UI process) and into CSS or WAAPI transforms and opacity. JS springs should be kept for gestures that track the finger 1:1, where 60 Hz is acceptable *[inference]*. Reduced motion is universal among the careful apps:
- YouVersion uses `motion-reduce:transition-none`.
- Substack sets its selection animation to `none`.
- Readwise's January 2026 release made modals, menus and horizontal pagination respect it.
- Libby adds its own in-app "Reduce Motion" setting.
- Apple Books drops even the cross-fade.

Pager already has `prefers-reduced-motion` blocks in [tokens.css](../src/styles/tokens.css) and [globals.css](../src/styles/globals.css). Any new motion should route through them.

## Opening a book exposes a web view unless the shell hides the seams

Libby, the largest web-based reader, hides its seams with choreography *[code]* ([Libby main.js](https://libbyapp.com/dewey-22.2.0/src/main.js)):
- **Cover zoom.** Opening a book finds the largest visible `[data-cover-id]` element, takes its `getBoundingClientRect()`, and calls `screen.emerge()` from that rect. The effect is a shared-element zoom from cover to book.
- **A separate reader webview.** In the native apps the reader runs in its own webview, which the shell reveals and conceals. Leaving the reader conceals it after a **100 ms** delay instead of destroying it.
- **Status-bar color.** A `colorize` bridge keeps a priority stack of color locks and computes HSLuv and YIQ values, so the shell can pick a matching status-bar tint.

Omnivore does the same at lower cost *[code]* ([WebReaderContainer.swift](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/mobile/apple/OmnivoreKit/Sources/App/Views/WebReader/WebReaderContainer.swift); [SwipeRightToPopViewController.swift](https://github.com/omnivore-app/omnivore/blob/main/mobile/apple/OmnivoreKit/Sources/App/Views/SwipeRightToPopViewController.swift)):
- **One shared WKWebView.** On exit it loads a theme-colored empty page, so the next book never flashes old content.
- **A soft loading state.** A progress view fades in while content loads.
- **Matching edges.** The scroll view's background and indicator style follow the theme.
- **Swipe back from anywhere.** A swipe-to-pop anywhere in the content commits at over 30% of the width or over 1000 pt/s, with an ease-out finish.

Apple Books' swipe-down-to-close (iOS 16–26) was disabled in scroll mode for the gesture-ownership reason given earlier *[native]* ([Apple, iOS 26 guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/read-books-iphc1af7c57/26/ios/26)). Readwise's changelog shows what happens without this choreography. In 2026 it fixed multi-screen startup flashes (replaced in October by a single launch screen) and back navigation that sometimes left a mostly blank screen. In 2024 it fixed top icons that overlapped the iOS status bar *[native]* ([Readwise changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)). Users notice. A forum user judged that "the design, relative to Matter, is just not as good in Reader" ([MacPowerUsers](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/any-one-using-matter-in-view-to-replace-instapaper-and-pocket-etc/27034?page=3)). Matter is praised as "my favorite app design on the App Store today" ([The Sweet Setup](https://thesweetsetup.com/is-matter-or-readwise-reader-the-read-later-app-for-you/)). Notion went further and moved its high-interaction screens to native code. On iOS that gave about 2× faster perceived startup ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/blog/notion-on-android-is-now-more-than-twice-as-fast-to-launch)).

The common web-view tells are well documented. WebKit's 350 ms tap delay is already gone for Pager, because a `width=device-width, initial-scale=1` viewport removes it ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/5610/more-responsive-tapping-on-iOS/)). The others need native settings:
- **Link previews.** `allowsLinkPreview` defaults to true, so a long press on a link "pops" the user into Safari ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/webkit/wkwebview/allowslinkpreview)).
- **Status-bar tap.** Scroll-to-top reaches only "the scroll view closest to the status bar". On iPhone it does nothing if more than one scroll view has `scrollsToTop` enabled ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiscrollview/scrollstotop)).
- **Whole-app rubber band.** A non-scrolling root still bounces through the native scroll view unless `bounces` is turned off *[inference]*.

**Building it in Pager.** [WebView.swift](../ios/Sources/Pager/WebView.swift) currently enables back-forward swipe gestures, sets `isOpaque = false` with `.systemBackground`, and configures neither `bounces` nor `allowsLinkPreview`. These changes **need native help**:
- set `scrollView.bounces = false` for the fixed root;
- set `allowsLinkPreview = false`;
- sync the background color with the theme through the bridge;
- add a status-bar-tap forwarder that posts `scrollToTop` to JS;
- set `CADisableMinimumFrameDurationOnPhone` for the native parts.

The open-book transition is web-only if the library and reader share one webview. Use View Transitions (Safari 18.0+, feature-detected, with a cross-fade fallback on 17.5) to emerge from the cover rect, as Libby does. If the reader ever becomes its own native screen, SwiftUI's iOS 18 `.navigationTransition(.zoom)` provides the zoom and the drag-to-dismiss.

## Conclusion

The evidence points to an uncomfortable truth for anyone tempted to start with animation. What users punish in public is not a missing flourish. They punish touches that do the wrong thing and controls they cannot find:
- Apple Books' accidental double-tap bookmarks: "about 75% of the bookmarks I made were accidental" ([BasicAppleGuy](https://basicappleguy.com/basicappleblog/build-a-better-books)).
- Libby's top-edge bookmarks ([Oak Park PL](https://www.oppl.org/news-events/digital-learning/tech-tips-how-to-read-ebooks-magazines-in-libby/)).
- Kindle's edge zones swallowing footnote links.
- Readwise's two-year run of "one tap, one action" fixes.
- Apple's own retreat from a button that could not be hidden.

The pleasant feel of these apps is mostly **arbitration**: deciding, within a few milliseconds and a few points of movement, whether a touch was a tap, a turn, a selection or a scroll. Pager should therefore build one gesture arbiter that every reader surface consults, in a fixed claim order like Readest's (edge brightness, then bookmark pull, then paginator), before tuning a single spring. The second insight is that the table stakes have converged: recency-first color, tap-to-cycle progress, a back control that appears only after a jump, and a live half-height settings panel. The remaining room to differentiate lies in ideas that only the smaller apps ship: Libby's distance-labeled chapter navigation, Readest's pull-to-bookmark and selection-corner page turn, and Kobo's pinch-to-resize.

Pager's hybrid architecture is not the handicap it looks like. Libby runs at library scale on the same shape, and Omnivore and Readest show that a thin Swift bridge closes most of the gap. The real ceilings are narrower. One is the 60 Hz `requestAnimationFrame` cap, which argues for native scroll snapping and CSS transforms over JS-driven motion. The other is the set of native-only surfaces: haptics, the edit menu, the status bar and brightness. The biggest remaining unknown is the native apps' own motion: their curl curves, sheet springs and haptic placements. A short 240 fps screen recording of Apple Books, Kindle and YouVersion on a device would settle it. The evidence suggests that matching them exactly matters less than never doing the wrong thing on a touch.

## What Pager should build, in priority order

### Do first: the feel fundamentals

1. **One gesture arbiter for the reader** (web-only). These rules apply to [PagedView.tsx](../src/components/reader/PagedView.tsx) and [ReaderBody.tsx](../src/components/reader/ReaderBody.tsx):
   - One tap does one thing.
   - A tap is dropped if the content scrolled during the touch, or if momentum ended within about 150 ms.
   - Links, highlights and footnotes win over edge zones.
   - Dismissing a popover never also toggles chrome or turns the page.
   - A long press never navigates.
   - Readest's center band (37.5–62.5%) toggles chrome, and while the chrome is visible the first tap only hides it.
   - Readest's thresholds apply: 15 px slop, 250 ms double-tap, 500 ms long press.

Two things should not be built: a true page curl, whose cost in native snapshotting is out of proportion to its value, and silent auto-archive or auto-finish at the end of a document.
